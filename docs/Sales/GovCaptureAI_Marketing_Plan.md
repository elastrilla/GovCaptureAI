# GovCaptureAI Marketing Plan

## Purpose

Sprint 12C prepares GovCaptureAI for real buyer conversations before Sprint 13A. The goal is not broad brand marketing yet. The goal is to line up enough qualified demos to learn whether small and mid-sized government contractors care about AI-assisted capture workflow.

## Market Thesis

GovCaptureAI should start with contractors who already pursue federal work but still manage early capture decisions through spreadsheets, email, SAM.gov searches, shared folders, and ad hoc proposal drafts.

The strongest early buyer promise:

> GovCaptureAI helps a contractor turn opportunity data into a clearer bid/no-bid decision and a faster first proposal draft.

## Market Anchors

- SBA contracting assistance programs support small-business participation in federal contracting and include 8(a), WOSB, SDVOSB, and HUBZone categories.
- SBA notes the federal government uses small-business contracting goals, including at least 23% of federal contracting dollars for small businesses.
- SAM.gov registration guidance points contractors toward Dynamic Small Business Search and APEX Accelerators as government contracting resources.
- GSA eLibrary is a practical public source for finding schedule holders, contractor categories, and potential GovCon companies.

Source URLs:

- https://www.sba.gov/federal-contracting/contracting-assistance-programs
- https://sam.gov/entity-registration
- https://www.gsaelibrary.gsa.gov/ElibMain/home.do

## Ideal Customer Profile

Primary ICP:

- Small or mid-sized federal contractor.
- Already registered or pursuing registration to do federal business.
- Has a capture, business development, proposal, or founder-led sales function.
- Reviews opportunities regularly from SAM.gov, GSA sources, agency forecasts, or partner alerts.
- Has enough past performance and capabilities to benefit from company-to-opportunity fit scoring.
- Feels pain around screening, bid/no-bid discipline, proposal starts, or reusable company knowledge.

Best early titles:

- Founder or CEO of a small GovCon firm.
- Capture manager.
- Business development manager.
- Proposal manager.
- Proposal consultant serving small contractors.
- GovCon advisor or fractional BD/capture lead.

## Priority Segments

### Segment 1: Small Federal IT And Cyber Contractors

Why they fit:

- They review high volumes of RFIs, RFQs, Sources Sought, and RFPs.
- They often have reusable capabilities and past performance.
- They can understand the value of AI-assisted qualification quickly.

Demo hook:

> "Let us show how your team could screen opportunities against your capabilities and generate a first response outline faster."

### Segment 2: 8(a), WOSB, SDVOSB, And HUBZone Contractors

Why they fit:

- Set-aside eligibility makes opportunity fit especially important.
- They often need disciplined pursuit decisions while still growing capture maturity.
- They may not have expensive enterprise capture software.

Demo hook:

> "GovCaptureAI can help document why a set-aside opportunity is or is not worth pursuing."

### Segment 3: Proposal Consultants And Capture Advisors

Why they fit:

- They influence multiple contractor clients.
- They know the pain of scattered capability evidence and rushed proposal starts.
- They can become referral partners even if they are not direct buyers.

Demo hook:

> "This could become a lightweight workflow for helping your clients move from opportunity review to draft readiness."

### Segment 4: Founder-Led GovCon Firms

Why they fit:

- Founders often make bid/no-bid decisions personally.
- They need repeatability but may not yet have a dedicated capture team.
- A focused pilot is easier to sell than enterprise software.

Demo hook:

> "This is a way to make pursuit decisions less dependent on memory and gut feel."

## Offer Ladder

Use the current pricing package as the sales motion:

1. Free 20-minute discovery/demo conversation.
2. Paid Discovery Sprint for buyers who need target-market clarity.
3. Focused Pilot as the primary offer.
4. Implementation Setup only after successful pilot validation.

Recommended first call CTA:

> "If this looks relevant, the next step is a focused pilot using your company profile and a real target market."

## 30-Day Demo Pipeline Plan

### Week 1: Prepare And Seed

- Finalize outreach copy.
- Build a 25-account starter list.
- Prioritize warm contacts, LinkedIn connections, APEX/SBDC contacts, GovCon associations, and proposal consultants.
- Use the prospect pipeline workbook to assign owner, segment, status, and next action.

### Week 2: First Outreach Wave

- Send 10 warm/referral messages.
- Send 10 targeted LinkedIn messages.
- Contact 3 referral partners, such as proposal consultants or GovCon advisors.
- Goal: book 3 demo conversations.

### Week 3: Demo And Follow-Up

- Run the first 2 to 3 demos.
- Capture feedback immediately after each demo.
- Ask each attendee for one referral to another contractor or proposal leader.
- Refine the demo talk track based on objections.

### Week 4: Second Outreach Wave

- Send follow-ups to non-responders.
- Add 25 more prospects from DSBS, GSA eLibrary, LinkedIn, and GovCon groups.
- Book 3 to 5 additional demos.
- Decide whether Sprint 13A has enough real feedback to begin.

## Target Metrics

Early goals:

- 50 researched prospects.
- 30 outbound messages.
- 8 positive replies.
- 5 scheduled demos.
- 3 completed demos.
- 2 serious pilot conversations.

Go/no-go for Sprint 13A:

- At least 3 completed demos with structured feedback.
- At least 1 buyer says the problem is urgent enough to discuss a pilot.
- At least 5 repeated pains or objections captured in the feedback tracker.

## Prospect Research Sources

Use these sources to build and verify the prospect list:

- Personal network and warm referrals.
- LinkedIn searches for capture manager, proposal manager, GovCon founder, federal contractor, 8(a), SDVOSB, WOSB, HUBZone.
- SBA Dynamic Small Business Search via SAM.gov resource links.
- GSA eLibrary contractor directory and category searches.
- APEX Accelerators and SBDC contacts for referral conversations.
- GovCon associations, chambers, webinars, and local meetups.
- Proposal consultants and capture advisors.

## Qualification Questions

Ask before or during the demo:

- How do you find opportunities today?
- How many opportunities do you review each month?
- Who decides bid/no-bid?
- What makes an opportunity hard to evaluate?
- What information do you reuse across proposals?
- What happens when a pursuit is rejected?
- What would make a pilot worth paying for?

## Core Messaging

Short positioning:

> GovCaptureAI helps small government contractors screen opportunities, explain bid/no-bid choices, manage early capture workflow, and generate stronger first-draft proposal content.

Pain-led message:

> Most teams can find opportunities. The hard part is deciding which ones deserve time and turning that decision into proposal momentum.

Pilot-led message:

> We can run a focused pilot against your company profile and target market to see whether AI-assisted capture workflow saves review time and improves decision quality.

## Risks

- The buyer may see this as "just another SAM.gov search tool."
- Contractors may want full proposal automation before trusting capture workflow value.
- Some buyers may not have clean company profile or past performance inputs.
- Cold outreach may underperform without warm referrals.

Mitigations:

- Lead with bid/no-bid and proposal readiness, not search.
- Use the live dashboard to show workflow, not just data.
- Offer Discovery Sprint when company inputs are messy.
- Ask every demo attendee for one referral.

## Sprint 13A Readiness

Move into Sprint 13A when the pipeline has enough real learning:

- 3 or more completed demos.
- 5 or more logged feedback items.
- Clear pattern of buyer pain.
- At least one concrete pilot candidate or referral path.
